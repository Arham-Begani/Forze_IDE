Forze Debug Rooms

Arham Begani · Solo student builder · Bengaluru, India

When a deployment fails in Forze, invite a teammate straight to the error. Debug it together in a browser and leave behind the fix that worked.

The problem

A developer pastes a build log into a team chat. The teammate asks which deployment it came from. Once someone fixes the build, the next developer has to reconstruct which reply helped and whether it ever shipped.

What a judge can do

Open a failing deployment, select safe diagnostic lines and review the exact excerpt. Invite one teammate into a browser room with the error above the conversation. Accept their reply, correct the import and rerun the build. Reopening the room shows the original diagnostic, the accepted suggestion and the observed rerun result independently.

Where Nexconn runs

The server gives each room two distinct, random Nexconn identities and signs a regional access-token request. Both browsers use the official Web Chat SDK to connect, send direct-channel messages, receive live events and fetch conversation history on refresh. The service stores the approved error, accepted reply and deployment observation. The App Secret stays on the server.

What existed before the challenge

Forze already had a Tauri and React editor, Supabase sign-in, Vercel deployment controls, a build-log client and a secret scanner. The new work is the error-sharing action, local selection and preview, browser room, invite and identity exchange, Nexconn conversation, accepted solution, rerun handoff and reproducible judge project.

Who would use it

Small teams and student builders can bring a reviewer into one deployment without giving them the project’s Vercel credentials. A pilot could measure how often invitations lead to a reply, how many fixes get a successful rerun, and whether teams open a second room. No traction figures or tester testimonials are claimed.

Required Nexconn App Key

Enter the same App Key in the submission form. The browser example runs a real local sample build. Vercel observations originate in the signed-in IDE.
